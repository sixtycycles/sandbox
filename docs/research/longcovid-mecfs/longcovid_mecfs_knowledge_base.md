# Long COVID (ME/CFS Subtype) — Knowledge Base

A curated, peer-reviewed corpus on the ME/CFS subtype of long COVID: pathophysiology/mechanisms, treatments & trials, and the long COVID ↔ ME/CFS overlap. Coverage spans foundational pre-COVID ME/CFS work (2014–2019) through current literature. Preprints excluded; observational work limited in favor of reviews, RCTs, and landmark mechanistic studies from high-impact journals.

**25 sources.** Companion SQLite DB: `longcovid_mecfs.db` (browse with Datasette).

## Overlap & Foundations — Long COVID ↔ ME/CFS, Case Definitions

### Long COVID: major findings, mechanisms and recommendations
*Davis HE, McCorkell L, Vogel JM, Topol EJ* — **Nature Reviews Microbiology** (2023) · Review · tier: flagship

The definitive high-level synthesis of long COVID, covering >200 symptoms across organ systems and the leading mechanistic hypotheses (viral persistence, autoimmunity, microclots, reactivated herpesviruses, dysbiosis). Explicitly frames a large long-COVID subset as meeting ME/CFS criteria and driven by post-exertional malaise. Sets research and clinical priorities; the single best entry point to the field.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1038/s41579-022-00846-2 · PMID 36639608

### Postacute sequelae of COVID-19 at 2 years
*Bowe B, Xie Y, Al-Aly Z* — **Nature Medicine** (2023) · Cohort (large-scale) · tier: flagship

Landmark large-scale cohort (VA database) quantifying long-term risk across 80 sequelae at two years post-infection. Shows persistent elevated risk especially in those hospitalized, with fatigue and neurologic outcomes prominent. Establishes the durability and population burden of the condition.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02521-2 · PMID 37656312

### Insights from myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome may help unravel the pathogenesis of postacute COVID-19 syndrome
*Komaroff AL, Lipkin WI* — **Trends in Molecular Medicine** (2021) · Review · tier: flagship

Two leading investigators argue that decades of ME/CFS research provide a ready-made framework for understanding long COVID, given the shared post-infectious onset, PEM, autonomic dysfunction and neuroinflammation. Reviews overlapping immune, metabolic and neurological abnormalities. A foundational conceptual bridge between the two conditions.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molmed.2021.06.002 · PMID 34175230

### Onset patterns and course of myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome
*Chu L, ... Montoya JG* — **Frontiers in Pediatrics** (2019) · Survey/cohort · tier: solid

Large patient survey characterizing how ME/CFS begins—most commonly after an infection—and its typically chronic, relapsing course. Documents the post-infectious onset pattern that makes ME/CFS the natural template for long COVID. Useful for natural-history context.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.3389/fped.2019.00012 · PMID 30805319

### Beyond myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome: redefining an illness (IOM report)
*Institute of Medicine (US) Committee* — **National Academies Press (IOM/NAM report)** (2015) · Consensus report / diagnostic criteria · tier: flagship

The landmark U.S. Institute of Medicine report that reviewed thousands of studies, affirmed ME/CFS as a serious physiological disease, and proposed new diagnostic criteria centered on post-exertional malaise (proposing the name 'systemic exertion intolerance disease'). The authoritative reference for case definition and legitimacy of the illness.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.17226/19012 · PMID 25695122

## Pathophysiology & Mechanisms

### Fibrin drives thromboinflammation and neuropathology in COVID-19 / fibrinaloid microclots
*Pretorius E, Kell DB, et al.* — **Journal of Medical Virology (assoc. microclot program)** (2025) · Case-control · tier: solid

Part of the influential microclot research program showing that anomalous fibrin amyloid ('fibrinaloid') microclots resistant to fibrinolysis are associated with neutrophil activation and persist in long COVID. Proposes impaired microcirculatory oxygen delivery as a driver of fatigue and PEM. A leading (though still-debated) vascular hypothesis.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1002/jmv.70351

### Muscle abnormalities worsen after post-exertional malaise in long COVID
*Appelman B, ... Wüst RCI* — **Nature Communications** (2024) · Case-control (exercise challenge) · tier: flagship

Landmark study biopsying muscle before and after exercise in long-COVID patients, showing that post-exertional malaise is accompanied by real skeletal-muscle pathology: mitochondrial dysfunction, microclots in muscle tissue, and fibre damage. Provides objective biological proof that PEM is not deconditioning or psychological. Directly relevant to the ME/CFS hallmark symptom.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44432-3 · PMID 38177109

### Distinguishing features of long COVID identified through immune profiling
*Klein J, Wood J, Jaycox JR, ... Iwasaki A* — **Nature** (2023) · Case-control (multiomics) · tier: flagship

Rigorous multiomic immunophenotyping distinguishing long-COVID patients from controls, identifying altered T-cell populations, elevated antibodies to reactivated herpesviruses (EBV), and notably lower morning cortisol. Provides some of the strongest objective biological correlates of the condition. A cornerstone mechanistic paper.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06651-y · PMID 37748514

### Deep Phenotyping of Neurologic Postacute Sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 Infection (NIH)
*Walitt B, ... Nath A* — **Neurology: Neuroimmunology & Neuroinflammation** (2023) · Deep-phenotyping case-control · tier: high

Intensive NIH inpatient study deeply phenotyping long-COVID patients with autonomic testing, immune assays and neuroimaging, finding autonomic dysregulation and immune differences rather than structural brain damage. Rigorous, controlled characterization of the neuro-immune phenotype.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1212/nxi.0000000000200097 · PMID 37949665

### WASF3 disrupts mitochondrial respiration and may mediate exercise intolerance in ME/CFS
*Hwang S, ... Hwang PM* — **PNAS** (2023) · Mechanistic / basic science · tier: flagship

NIH cardiology lab traces exercise intolerance in an ME/CFS patient to overexpression of the protein WASF3, which impairs mitochondrial supercomplex assembly and respiration. Reproduces the defect in cell and mouse models. A rare direct molecular mechanism linking a specific protein to the core energy-metabolism defect.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2306090120 · PMID 37549300

### SARS-CoV-2 infection and persistence in the human body and brain at autopsy
*Stein SR, Ramelli SC, ... Chertow DS (NIH)* — **Nature** (2022) · Autopsy study · tier: flagship

Comprehensive autopsy study demonstrating that SARS-CoV-2 RNA can persist in multiple tissues, including brain, for months after infection. Provides direct physical evidence for the viral-persistence hypothesis underlying long COVID. Key anatomical support for reservoir-driven chronic illness.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05542-y · PMID 36517603

### Immunological dysfunction persists for 8 months following initial mild-to-moderate SARS-CoV-2 infection
*Phetsouphanh C, ... Matthews GV* — **Nature Immunology** (2022) · Longitudinal cohort · tier: flagship

Demonstrates sustained immune activation—elevated type I/III interferons and other cytokines—persisting eight months after even mild infection in those with long COVID. Establishes chronic immune dysregulation as a durable, measurable feature. Frequently cited mechanistic anchor.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1038/s41590-021-01113-x · PMID 35105985

### Immunoglobulin signature predicts risk of post-acute COVID-19 syndrome
*Cervia C, ... Boyman O* — **Nature Communications** (2022) · Prospective cohort · tier: high

Identifies a blood immunoglobulin signature (low IgM/IgG3) present during acute infection that predicts who later develops long COVID. One of the first prospective predictive biomarkers. Suggests intrinsic immune predisposition rather than purely post-hoc damage.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-27797-1 · PMID 35075108

### Nervous system consequences of COVID-19
*Spudich S, Nath A* — **Science** (2022) · Review · tier: flagship

Authoritative review of how SARS-CoV-2 affects the nervous system, spanning acute neurological injury through the neuroinflammation, autonomic dysfunction and cognitive impairment ('brain fog') of long COVID. Connects mechanisms to the neuro-symptoms shared with ME/CFS.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abm2052 · PMID 35025660

### Insights from invasive cardiopulmonary exercise testing / Persistent exertional intolerance after COVID-19
*Singh I, Joseph P, ... Systrom DM* — **Chest** (2022) · Physiology case series (iCPET) · tier: high

Invasive cardiopulmonary exercise testing in long-COVID patients reveals impaired systemic oxygen extraction and preload failure rather than cardiopulmonary limitation—mirroring findings in ME/CFS. Points to peripheral/mitochondrial and autonomic dysfunction as the basis of exercise intolerance. Rigorous mechanistic physiology.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2021.08.010 · PMID 34389297

### A map of metabolic phenotypes in patients with myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome
*Hoel F, ... Tronstad KJ / Fluge O* — **JCI Insight** (2021) · Case-control (metabolomics) · tier: high

Large metabolomic and functional study identifying impaired pyruvate dehydrogenase / amino-acid metabolism and reduced cellular energy production in ME/CFS, with evidence of a circulating serum factor affecting energy metabolism. Strengthens the mitochondrial/metabolic-dysfunction model with functional assays.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.149217 · PMID 34423789

### Cytokine signature associated with disease severity in chronic fatigue syndrome patients
*Montoya JG, ... Davis MM* — **PNAS** (2017) · Case-control (immune profiling) · tier: flagship

Large study (192 patients) finding that although mean cytokine levels differed little from controls, 17 cytokines correlated with ME/CFS severity, several pro-inflammatory. Reframed the illness as one of cytokine dysregulation scaling with severity rather than simple elevation. Landmark immunology paper from a Stanford group.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1710519114 · PMID 28760971

### Metabolic features of chronic fatigue syndrome
*Naviaux RK, ... Gordon E* — **PNAS** (2016) · Case-control (metabolomics) · tier: flagship

Seminal metabolomics study finding that ME/CFS patients share a hypometabolic 'dauer'-like state—a coordinated down-regulation across ~20 metabolic pathways resembling an evolutionarily conserved survival response. Proposed a unifying metabolic model and potential diagnostic signature. Highly influential despite subsequent calls for replication.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1607571113 · PMID 27573827

### Neuroinflammation in patients with chronic fatigue syndrome/ME: a PET study
*Nakatomi Y, ... Watanabe Y* — **Journal of Nuclear Medicine** (2014) · Neuroimaging case-control · tier: high

First PET imaging evidence of neuroinflammation in ME/CFS, showing elevated microglial/astrocyte activation in multiple brain regions correlating with symptom severity. Provided objective in-vivo support for the neuroinflammatory hypothesis. Small sample but frequently cited and field-shaping.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.113.131045 · PMID 24665088

## Treatments & Clinical Trials

### Neither metformin nor ursodeoxycholic acid effectively treats postacute sequelae of COVID-19 (RCT)
*(ACTIV-6 / platform trial investigators)* — **Annals of Internal Medicine** (2026) · RCT (platform) · tier: high

Rigorous randomized platform trial testing repurposed drugs for long COVID, finding neither metformin nor ursodeoxycholic acid improved symptoms versus placebo. High-quality negative evidence that narrows the repurposed-drug search space and illustrates the methodological bar for the field.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.7326/annals-25-04883 · PMID 41771135

### Evaluation of interventions for cognitive symptoms in long COVID: a randomized clinical trial
*(Long COVID cognition trial investigators)* — **JAMA Neurology** (2026) · RCT · tier: high

Randomized trial in a high-impact neurology journal evaluating interventions for the cognitive ('brain fog') symptoms of long COVID. Provides controlled efficacy data for cognitive-symptom management, an area otherwise dominated by low-quality evidence. Useful benchmark for what does/doesn't help cognition.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2025.4415 · PMID 41212544

### Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome: diagnosis and management
*(Consensus clinical review authors)* — **Journal of Translational Medicine** (2025) · Clinical review · tier: solid

Contemporary peer-reviewed clinical review synthesizing evidence-based diagnosis (IOM/Canadian criteria) and management of ME/CFS, emphasizing pacing/energy management, orthostatic-intolerance treatment, sleep and symptom-targeted pharmacology—and cautioning against graded exercise. A practical, guideline-oriented anchor for the management section.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-025-07506-y · PMID 41366804

### Six-year follow-up of participants in two clinical trials of rituximab or cyclophosphamide in ME/CFS
*Rekeland IG, ... Fluge Ø* — **PLoS ONE** (2024) · Long-term follow-up · tier: solid

Long-term follow-up of the Norwegian rituximab/cyclophosphamide cohorts, reporting sustained improvement in a subset (notably cyclophosphamide) despite the negative primary rituximab RCT. Keeps the immunomodulation question open for a responder subgroup while cautioning on toxicity.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0307484 · PMID 39106264

### A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase III trial of rintatolimod (Ampligen) in chronic fatigue syndrome / effect of disease duration
*Strayer DR, ... Mitchell WM* — **PLoS ONE (Phase III program)** (2020) · RCT (phase III, secondary analysis) · tier: solid

Analysis from the phase-III program of rintatolimod (a TLR3 agonist immunomodulator, the only near-approved ME/CFS drug) showing exercise-tolerance benefit concentrated in patients with shorter disease duration. Supports early-intervention windows; the drug remains unapproved by the FDA pending further data.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0240403 · PMID 33064751

### B-lymphocyte depletion in patients with myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
*Fluge Ø, ... Mella O* — **Annals of Internal Medicine** (2019) · RCT (phase III) · tier: flagship

The definitive multicenter phase-III RCT of rituximab (B-cell depletion) in ME/CFS: it found NO benefit over placebo, overturning promising earlier open-label results. Critically important as high-quality negative evidence that tempered the autoimmune-B-cell hypothesis and underscored the placebo-response problem in the field.

🔗 https://doi.org/10.7326/m18-1451 · PMID 30934066

## How to browse the database

```bash
pip install datasette
datasette longcovid_mecfs.db
# then open http://localhost:8001
```

Schema: `sources(id, theme, title, authors, journal, year, doi, pmid, url, impact_tier, study_type, summary)`

_Note on rigor: DOIs verified against Europe PMC. 'flagship' = Nature/Science/Nature Medicine/PNAS/Nature Reviews/Nature Immunology/JAMA-tier or the IOM consensus report; 'high'/'solid' denote strong specialist journals. Two negative RCTs (rituximab; metformin/UDCA) are included deliberately — high-quality null results are core to an honest evidence base._
